# English Teaching Brand & Marketing Project — Working Context

*Finalized 24 August 2026. Incorporates all decisions from planning sessions.*

---

## 1. Project goal

Build Anna's English-teaching personal brand and a practical acquisition system that brings in paying students, starting with teenagers and later developing the adult segment.

The priority is **action over prolonged strategic research**. We already have strong hypotheses based on Anna's teaching experience and direct contact with teenagers, parents, and adult learners. We will validate these hypotheses through launch data, conversations, applications, trials, sales, and retention rather than spending weeks on formal market research.

The business should eventually evolve beyond lessons into a broader language-learning brand and, later, language products/apps informed by AI/GEO optimisation.

---

## 2. Brand positioning

Anna is an English teacher with a developer/IT background.

Important differentiator:
- English teacher since 2015
- CELTA certified
- Developer / works in tech
- Understands both language learning and the tech environment
- Personally learns languages and experiments with learning methods

### Brand architecture

**Instagram / acquisition level:** English for tech careers. This is the hook — fast, specific, credible. Combined with "Developer + CELTA teacher" it creates an immediately clear positioning.

**Broader brand territory:** Language as a tool for thinking, communicating and connecting. English is not always presented as the final goal. It is a tool people use to express ideas, think critically, communicate, connect with other people, and participate in work, culture and society.

**Key principle:** Tech is the acquisition hook, not the curriculum prison. Content attracts through tech/games/interviews. Inside the products, topics are broader: music, science, society, books, films, future jobs, current events — but tech remains a recurring lens because Anna genuinely lives in that world.

### Advertising constraints (Russian law)

Since September 2025, Russian law (Federal Law №72-FZ) prohibits advertising on Meta platforms. This applies to Russian citizens even abroad if content is accessible to users in Russia.

**Practical approach for Anna:**
- **Instagram:** No prices, no promotional language ("buy now," "sign up today," "special offer"). Neutral descriptions of services are allowed. All commercial CTAs point to external platforms.
- **Telegram:** Full commercial information allowed. FAS has a non-enforcement transition period through end of 2026 for Telegram.
- **auruby.io:** Anna's own domain, no jurisdictional restrictions. Full prices, CTAs, signup flows.
- **Google Forms:** Internal tool. Prices in form header — no restrictions.

## 4. Primary audience: teenagers

### Product: Teen English Club

Tagline (product-level, not brand-level):

> **Speak. Think. Connect. In English.**

Concept: A small online English community for curious teenagers where they use English to discuss interesting ideas, express opinions, challenge each other, listen to others and connect with peers. The club should feel like a place teenagers genuinely want to come to, not like another school lesson.

### Specs

- Ages: 13–17
- English: B1–C1
- Group: 5–6 students
- Online
- 2 × 60-minute sessions per week
- €10/session → **€80/month** (8 sessions)
- First session free

### Unit economics

- €80/student × 5 students = €400/month per group
- €80/student × 6 students = €480/month per group
- Two groups = €800–960/month
- Teaching load: 4 hours/week per group, 8 hours/week for two groups

### Free trial and group formation

Recruit ~10 students for a free first session. Expect 5–7 to continue. Split into groups by approximate level. If more than 6 want to continue, form two groups.

### What the teenager develops

- Speaking confidence
- Ability to express opinions
- Ability to formulate and defend ideas
- Discussion and debate skills
- Listening and interaction
- Vocabulary in meaningful contexts
- Confidence communicating with peers
- Social connection / community

### Teaching philosophy

English is the medium through which teenagers do something meaningful.

Discussion material includes: technology, AI, games, science, internet culture, music, films, books, future jobs, social questions, interesting current topics. Literary texts can also be used. Anna is NOT positioning as a literature teacher; texts are tools for language, thinking and discussion.

Important:
- Do not market this as "less screen time" — the club is online.
- Better framing: turn passive digital interests into active thinking, speaking and interaction.
- Technology is a tool/topic, not the ultimate educational outcome.

### Buyer journey

Both teenagers and parents matter.

Hypothesis: Teenagers discover Anna through Instagram, become interested, and ask their parents to try the club. The landing page must speak to BOTH:
- teenager: "This looks interesting and I want to be there."
- parent: "This is educational, purposeful, safe, and worth paying for."

Do not make the teenager passive or frame the product as something parents simply impose on them.

### Future: offline channel

**Oodi library (Helsinki)** as a venue for free introductory sessions. Non-commercial activity is allowed. Works as a lead magnet — attendees convert to online groups. Also generates content (photos, video). Launch after online groups are running.

Later: rent a studio space for paid offline groups if local demand proves sufficient (Helsinki/Espoo is the hottest local market in Finland).

## 5. Secondary audience: adults

### Product 1 — Speaking Reset

Working promise:

> **From understanding English to expressing yourself.**

Target: Adults B1–C1 who understand English but struggle to speak/write naturally and confidently.

Typical problems:
- "I understand English, but I can't speak."
- Translating in their head
- Difficulty formulating complex thoughts
- Lack of confidence
- Passive vocabulary much stronger than active vocabulary
- Living abroad / preparing to live abroad
- Need English for work or everyday communication

### Key differentiator: Point A → Point B

Anna does NOT sell vague "English practice." Before/at the beginning of the programme, she identifies where the learner is now, what they struggle with, what they need to train, and what situations they need English for. Then she creates a concrete plan.

The learner knows: what they are going to work on, why, what progress looks like, what result they are working toward.

This comes from Anna's personal frustration learning Finnish without a clear path.

### Speaking Reset specs

- 1:1, online
- 4 × 60 minutes
- €200
- Free diagnostic / initial assessment
- Personal English Action Plan
- Targeted training and practice
- Clear expected outcome

### Product 2 — Continuation

After Speaking Reset:

- ~€160–200/month
- Ongoing individual lessons
- Continued speaking practice
- Targeted correction
- Real-life situations
- Progress toward new goals

The aim is recurring revenue, not isolated hourly lessons.

### Standalone individual sessions

- €40/hour — general English
- €60/hour — interview prep, presentation coaching, high-stakes situations

### Tech/career angle

Anna's IT background is a strong differentiator, but adult English is NOT restricted exclusively to IT. Use cases include: international work, meetings, interviews, presentations, explaining technical ideas, communicating with international colleagues, relocation, everyday life abroad.

## 6. Content pillars

Four fixed content pillars. One post per pillar per week = 4 posts/week target.

### 🎮 THE GAME
Games + English. Use games as a source of vocabulary, expressions, discussion, opinions, cultural/contextual language.

### 📰 THE FEED
Tech news + English. Use current technology stories as a source of vocabulary, language, opinions, critical thinking, discussion.

### 🎙 THE TAPE
Real speech + English. Break down interviews, podcasts, conference talks. Focus on useful language, chunks, expressions, how people actually communicate, ideas worth discussing.

### 🧪 THE LAB
How we learn languages. Anna documents her own language-learning journey (Finnish, German, Romanian). Demonstrate methodology, show effective learning from the learner's perspective, build authority. NOT simply "watch me learn languages."

### Advertising weight per pillar

- **The Lab:** Light. Credibility-first. End with mirror to student experience + soft mention of services.
- **The Feed:** Direct. Post proves you belong in their world → clear offer at the end.
- **The Game:** Parent-facing through the teen. Fun post → "groups starting September → link in bio."
- **The Tape:** Sharpest. Interview dissection → "If you freeze when the stakes are real — that's what I do."

## 7. Marketing funnel

### Pricing architecture

Prices are NOT shown on Instagram (Russian advertising law constraint).

Prices are visible on:
- **Google Form header** (signup form)
- **auruby.io** (landing page)
- **Telegram channel** (full commercial info)

### Two paths to signup

**Quick path:** Instagram → profile → link in bio → Google Form (prices in header, signup)

**Long path:** Instagram → profile → link in bio → auruby.io (full info, prices) → Google Form (signup)

Both end at the same Google Form.

### Teen funnel

Instagram / content → profile → pinned post (Teen Club) → link in bio → landing page / Google Form → free trial → parent confirmation → payment → club → retention / referrals

### Adult funnel

Instagram / content → profile → pinned post (Adults) → link in bio → landing page / Google Form → free diagnostic → Personal English Action Plan → payment → 4-week programme → continuation

### Landing page philosophy

The landing page is not primarily an "about Anna" website. It should: communicate the idea/product immediately, make the visitor recognise the problem, show the solution, explain how it works, demonstrate why Anna is credible, show price, provide a clear CTA, move the visitor into an application.

## 9. Content and brand philosophy

The account should feel like a media/education brand, not a generic English-teacher account.

Tone: intelligent, curious, slightly cheeky / edgy, practical, modern, human.

Avoid generic ESL content.

The content should make people think: "This is interesting." → "I want to talk about this." → "I want to learn with her."

## 11. Current strategic principle

> **Build fast. Measure. Learn. Improve.**

We do not need to know everything before launch. We need a strong enough hypothesis, a real offer, a working funnel, traffic, and data.

Bio hypothesis: "English for tech careers" — test for 4–6 weeks, then review against profile visits, follows, link clicks, applications, and who actually comes. Adjust if data shows a different positioning works better.
